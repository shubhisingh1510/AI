01 Research Question & Motivation

Chronic lower-limb ulcers — venous, arterial, and diabetic/neuropathic — are managed very differently, but visually distinguishing them from a photograph alone is difficult even for clinicians in some cases, and the arterial-versus-diabetic distinction in particular is noted in the literature as a hard case. This project asks two linked questions:

  1. Can a deep-learning image classifier reliably distinguish between specific lower-limb wound etiologies from a photograph, rather than only detecting "wound present"?
  2. Does a hybrid classical–quantum classifier (a small variational quantum circuit on top of extracted image features) provide any measurable advantage over a matched classical baseline on the same task — or does it not, which is an equally valid and reportable outcome?

The project is explicitly designed to report the honest result either way: the evaluate_compare.py script in the repository is written to never describe a statistically non-significant difference between the classical and quantum models as an "improvement" for either side.

04 Preprocessing & Experimental Design verified

Splitting

Because no patient-ID mapping exists, src/data_prep.py falls back to a stratified image-level split (70/15/15 train/val/test, plus stratified 5-fold CV) and explicitly records split_method: stratified_image_level_fallback in every results file, so this limitation travels with every downstream number rather than being forgotten after the split step.

Group-safe split (current, used for all §7 results): bundles two fixes. First, a verified bug fix: the fallback pseudo-patient-ID above used to key on bare filename, which collides across class folders in this dataset (e.g. test_100_0.jpg exists under both diabetic/ and pressure/), silently forcing 191 of 559 fallback groups in the previously-published split to span more than one true class. Now keyed on "label/filename" instead, which is unique per image. Second, a more conservative addition: src/dedupe_and_group_split.py also groups visually near-duplicate images (flagged by the audit in §3) so every image in a group lands on the same side of every split. Together these changed 406 of 891 images' split assignment. The filename-collision fix is the well-evidenced explanation for the changed classical-vs-quantum result in §7 — see that section for why the near-duplicate grouping is not claimed as the cause. Every group-safe result file records split_method: dedupe_group_level.

Preprocessing & augmentation

  • Resize to 224×224, ImageNet mean/std normalization
  • Training-time augmentation: random resized crop (0.8–1.0 scale), horizontal flip, vertical flip (p=0.2), color jitter (brightness/contrast/saturation 0.2, hue 0.02)
  • No augmentation that could plausibly alter medically meaningful characteristics (e.g. no aggressive color/hue shifts that would distort wound-tissue color, which is diagnostically relevant)

Class imbalance

Both the classical and quantum training scripts use inverse-frequency class-weighted cross-entropy loss, recomputed from whichever training split is actually in use so each cross-validation fold gets weights matching its own class balance rather than one fixed weighting applied everywhere.

05 Model Architecture

Classical baseline verified

ResNet-50 (ImageNet-pretrained), classifier head replaced for 4 classes. Trained in two phases: backbone frozen for the first 5 epochs (head-only warmup), then unfrozen for full fine-tuning at a lower learning rate.

Hybrid classical–quantum classifier verified

Two feature-encoding strategies feed the same 6-qubit variational circuit (StronglyEntanglingLayers, PennyLane-Lightning, analytic adjoint gradients), and both were actually trained and evaluated rather than assumed:

  • PCA encoding (default) — the frozen classical ResNet-50's penultimate-layer features, PCA-reduced from 2048 to 6 dimensions (fit on the training split only), angle-embedded into the circuit. This is the "quantum transfer learning" pattern and reuses the exact same frozen CNN backbone as the classical baseline, so the comparison in §7 is apples-to-apples on the same learned representation.
  • Domain encoding (CNN-free alternative) — six clinically-motivated features computed directly from raw pixels: mean hue, hue variance, mean saturation, a redness index (erythema proxy), a texture-energy roughness proxy, and Canny edge density.

The qubit-count × circuit-depth ablation sweep (src/ablation.py, §7) showed the domain encoding tops out around 37% test accuracy — barely above the 25% random baseline for 4 classes — while PCA encoding reaches 70–82% depending on the split. PCA encoding is therefore the configured default (configs/config.yaml); domain encoding remains available as an explicit, CNN-free alternative for anyone who wants to avoid depending on the classical backbone entirely, but it is not competitive as a classifier on this dataset.

Matched-capacity classical control verified — new

Same frozen backbone and 6-dimensional PCA input as the quantum-hybrid's PCA path above, but a small classical 2-layer MLP head in place of the quantum circuit, with hidden width chosen so its parameter count (81) matches the quantum circuit's (82) as closely as possible — see src/classical_frozen_head.py. Exists to separate two competing explanations for any classical-vs-quantum accuracy gap: "frozen CNN features generalize better on a small dataset than full fine-tuning" versus "the quantum circuit itself is doing something better than a classical head would." Results in §7.

The classical baseline above fully fine-tunes the ResNet-50 backbone (23.5M trainable parameters), while the quantum-hybrid classifier below only ever trains a tiny head on top of a frozen backbone (82 parameters). Data re-uploading, tested and rejected: re-injecting the input encoding before every entangling layer (Perez-Salinas et al. 2020) — a standard way to increase a variational circuit's effective capacity without more qubits — was tried on this same split as a direct test of whether "more QNN expressiveness" closes any of the gap above. It does not: accuracy fell to 50.4% test / 62.3%±5.3% CV. With ~712 training images per fold, the extra re-injected parameters appear to make optimization harder without adding usable capacity. Reported as a genuine negative result: circuit expressiveness is not the bottleneck here, and doubling down on it makes things worse, not better.

Included specifically to show why this report reports both tests rather than only the one that would have supported a stronger claim.

Why the earlier "significant quantum win" doesn't survive scrutiny — two independent reasons, not one:

  1. The statistical test itself was too generous. Re-running the significance test on the original, unchanged 730-image CV results with the Nadeau-Bengio corrected resampled t-test (added this session; see §7 methodology list) turns the originally reported p=0.0345 into p=0.104 (not significant) — with no split change at all. The naive test's known bias (Dietterich, 1998) toward overstating significance on overlapping CV folds was, on its own, enough to make the original claim not hold up.
  2. A real, verified split bug was also found and fixed. data_prep.py's fallback pseudo-patient-ID (used when no real patient-ID file exists) keyed on bare filename, which collides across class folders in this dataset (e.g. test_100_0.jpg exists under both diabetic/ and pressure/). This silently forced 191 of 559 fallback pseudo-patient groups in the previously-published split to span more than one true class label — a confirmed code bug, not a probabilistic inference. Fixing it (keying on "label/filename" instead) changed 406 of 891 images' split assignment and is the most likely dominant cause of the numeric changes above.

The group-safe split (§4) also clusters visually near-duplicate images (flagged by the audit in §3) so they can't be split across train/test, as an additional, more conservative safeguard bundled into the same fix. A small manual spot-check of 2 of the 35 flagged cross-class pairs (viewing the actual images, not just the hash distance) found both to be visually distinct wounds — different anatomy, framing, and appearance — suggesting many of these hash matches are false positives of the coarse 8×8 average-hash rather than genuine near-duplicates. This report does not claim the near-duplicate grouping itself is what changed the result (that claim was not verified and the limited spot-check argues against it); the filename-collision bug above is the well-evidenced explanation. Keeping the near-duplicate groups together remains a reasonable conservative default regardless, and the full 35 pairs have not yet been individually reviewed (see §9).

Read together, honestly: there is no statistically robust evidence, on the corrected split, that the quantum circuit outperforms a classical model of any kind, and the original "significant" claim was already fragile under a more appropriate statistical test even before any split change. The one place quantum still looks favorable is CV variance (±2.1%, the lowest of the three) and inference latency (7.4ms vs. classical's 97.1ms) — real, measurable properties, just not "higher accuracy."

Qubit × circuit-depth ablation from the prior (pre-correction) dataset

src/ablation.py swept {4, 6, 8} qubits × {1, 2, 3} circuit-depth layers for both feature encodings on the earlier 730-image split (this sweep has not yet been rerun on the group-safe split; see §10):

  • Domain encoding never exceeds 41.6% CV-mean accuracy at any qubit/depth setting tried — it stays close to the 25% random baseline for 4 classes across the entire grid, which is why it was not selected as the configured default despite being the CNN-free option.
  • PCA encoding's best point in that grid was 6 qubits / depth 3 (82.5% CV-mean accuracy) — the setting used for the group-safe reruns above. Accuracy generally increased with circuit depth at fixed qubit count for the PCA encoding, but did not increase monotonically with qubit count.

Evaluation methodology, summarized

Every claim above is backed by more than one of the following, deliberately — no single metric or test carries the whole argument:

  1. Accuracy, precision, recall, F1 (per-class and macro-averaged)
  2. Confusion matrices (per model)
  3. ROC-AUC, one-vs-rest macro-averaged
  4. 5-fold cross-validation (not a single train/test split)
  5. Naive paired t-test and the Nadeau-Bengio (2003) corrected resampled paired t-test on CV fold accuracy (the corrected test exists specifically because the naive one is known to overstate significance — see the classical-vs-frozen-head result above for a case where that distinction changes the conclusion)
  6. McNemar's exact test on paired test-set predictions
  7. A matched-capacity classical control, to separate "frozen features" from "quantum circuit" as competing explanations for any accuracy difference
  8. Qubit-count × circuit-depth × data-re-uploading ablation sweep
  9. Grad-CAM explainability (§8)
  10. Efficiency comparison: trainable parameters, training time, inference latency
  11. Multimodal (image+location) fusion, as an independent check of whether the bottleneck is model capacity or missing information
  12. Test-time augmentation, as a no-retraining robustness check

09 Limitations

  • No arterial class. The model cannot recognize arterial ulcers at all — it was never trained on any. This is the single biggest scope gap relative to the original three-way research question.
  • Patient-level leakage still cannot be fully ruled out. AZH ships no patient-ID mapping. The group-safe split (§7) does deduplicate near-identical images across classes, which removes one real leakage channel that the original split had, but it cannot guarantee two visually-different images of the same patient stay in the same split.
  • Two-source dataset, still small. 891 images across 4 classes: 730 from one center (AZH, Milwaukee WI) plus a 161-image Medetec supplement added to improve class balance (1.84x → 1.51x). No evidence on generalization to other cameras, lighting, or patient populations, and combining two sources introduces its own (unquantified) distribution-shift risk between them.
  • No image-only model in this study reaches high accuracy. Best 5-fold CV mean among the image-only models on the full 891-image set is 79.5% (the matched-capacity classical control, §7); test-set accuracy tops out at 72.9%. Two direct attempts to get more out of the quantum circuit specifically (the qubit/depth ablation grid, and re-uploading) both failed to help — evidence that circuit/hyperparameter tuning is not the bottleneck. What did help: adding wound body-location as a second input modality (§7's fusion result, 83.0%±3.0% CV) — but only on the 730-image subset that has real location labels, not the full 891. Closing that gap for the other 161 images (or finding a second dataset with both images and location) is the most promising concrete next step for further accuracy gains, more so than further model tuning on images alone.
  • Not a diagnostic tool. This is a research prototype. No claim here should be read as validated for clinical decision-making.
